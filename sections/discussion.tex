The results reveal no single dominant configuration, and instead each approach demonstrates distinct trade-offs between plan generation, refusal accuracy, and token cost. The iterative approach achieves the highest rate of successful plan generation on solvable problems and the lowest token cost, as its lower bound is a single LLM query. However, this comes at the cost of lower refusal accuracy. Applications where false positives are dangerous should favor consensus- or judge-based approaches, which preserve refusal accuracy with little sacrifice in plan-generation performance.


Model selection has a meaningful impact beyond raw accuracy. Across the board, Gemma4 outperforms DeepSeek with CAST. Thinking-enabled models (\textsc{DS}, \textsc{G4}) are more willing to make creative type assignments, which benefits solvable problems but undermines refusal on unsolvable ones. \textsc{G4NT} presents a different profile: it differs little from \textsc{G4} on recovery, but, being more conservative and literal, offers substantially better refusal behavior and, lacking a reasoning trace, lower token cost. This suggests that model choice should be informed by the application's tolerance for invalid substitutions. An interesting direction for future work would be a mixture-of-models consensus that combines the creative reasoning of thinking-enabled models with the conservatism of \textsc{G4NT} to improve both plan generation and refusal.
