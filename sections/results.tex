\paragraph{Single-Substitution Recovery and Baseline Comparison (\textit{RQ1}, \textit{RQ6}).}
 On single-substitution problems CAST recovers near-perfectly: the Gemma4 variants reach $100\%$ and Deepseek is close behind. Both unstructured baselines are weaker and far less consistent. Constraining the LLM to a validated type assignment therefore recovers substitutions more reliably than free-form PDDL rewriting (direct) or end-to-end planning (APB).

\paragraph{Simultaneous Substitutions (\textit{RQ2}).}Bulk problems are the most difficult configuration across all evaluation families, with recovery ranging from $38\%$ to $68\%$ with substantial variation. CAST achieves the highest recovery under each model, although G4-Direct ties the best G4 CAST result at $60\%$. Because several substitutions are required simultaneously, every assignment must be valid for the plan to succeed. Even G4-Direct falls from $100\%$ on single-substitution problems to $60\%$ on bulk, while the best overall result is only $68\%$. This consistent decline indicates that the primary difficulty is obtaining multiple correct substitutions simultaneously.

% \textit{RQ2}: \textsc{bulk} problems, which require several simultaneous substitutions, are the hardest category for every method, with the strongest configurations plateauing near $60\%$ at high variance. Every required substitution must be correct at once for the plan to succeed, so a single wrong assignment among several is enough to fail the whole problem; even \textsc{G4} under the \emph{direct} baseline, which reaches $100\%$ on \textsc{single}, falls to the same $\sim\!60\%$ range on \textsc{bulk}, showing this is a property of the task rather than of any particular querying strategy.

\paragraph{Robustness Across Semantic Domains (\textit{RQ5}).}The semantic-domain columns of Table~\ref{tab:refusal} report single-substitution recovery across semantic domains. The G4 and G4NT consensus and iterative configurations remain between $98\%$ and $100\%$ across all four domains, demonstrating strong transfer across semantic contexts. The judge variants and DeepSeek configurations show greater sensitivity, particularly in the kitchen domain. 
% Baseline performance is more model-dependent: G4-Direct achieves $100\%$, whereas the other Direct configurations range from $70\%$ to $98\%$, and APB ranges from $32\%$ to $100\%$. Thus, constrained typing improves cross-domain consistency, though it does not outperform every baseline configuration.

\paragraph{Refusal Accuracy (\textit{RQ4}).}Correct refusal is critical for deployment because a planner-compatible but functionally invalid substitution can produce ineffective or unsafe behavior. Judge achieves the highest refusal accuracy across all models, averaging $76.7\%$, compared with $0.7\%$ for Direct and $8.0\%$ for APB. Consensus averages $55.3\%$, while iterative refinement reaches only $20.0\%$. The baselines usually propose invalid substitutions rather than returning no plan, demonstrating the value of explicit semantic evaluation. Iterative refinement repeatedly revises assignments after planner failures, increasing the likelihood of a solvable but invalid assignment. Even Judge fails to refuse in roughly one-quarter of cases, leaving reliable refusal an open challenge and exposing a clear recovery--refusal trade-off.

\paragraph{Token Usage.}Figure~\ref{fig:token_usage} reports average input and output token usage per problem. Consensus is the most expensive CAST variant because it always issues $n$ independent queries. Judge is cheaper, requiring in the best case a single typing and judge query per round, while iterative refinement is the least expensive CAST variant because at best it terminates after a single initial query. Direct and APB have the lowest overall token usage.

% \textit{RQ4}: Table \ref{tab:refusal} reports the refusal accuracy of CAST compared with the baselines, and we observe that a structured approach like ours has an advantage over the unstructured baseline methods. Both baselines never refuse -- more specifically, the direct PDDL-repair baseline declines on just $0.7\%$ of unsolvable problems on average and APB on $8\%$ --- because both provide invalid substitutions rather than refusing to solve. CAST, however, allows for the LLM to leave pre-existing type mappings in the case that no substitution exists. Among the CAST variants, the judge mode refuses most reliably ($77\%$ average), iterative collapses ($20\%$, as low as $2\%$ for \textsc{G4}) because it loops until a plan is found which often leads the LLM to propose invalid substitutions, and consensus is intermediate. No method refuses perfectly, exposing a recovery--refusal trade-off: the same willingness that recovers hard substitutions also accepts invalid ones.

Consensus is the most expensive CAST variant, as it always issues $n$ independent queries (shown here at $n{=}3$; token usage for additional values of $n$ is available in the Appendix). Comparatively, the judge mode is cheaper as it only requires a single typing query along with a lightweight judge call, plus occasional re-queries when the judge is unsatisfied. The iterative approach is the cheapest of the CAST variants despite its looping behavior: its lower bound is a single LLM query, since a valid plan after the first iteration requires no further calls. The direct and APB baselines are the cheapest of the tested methods.
